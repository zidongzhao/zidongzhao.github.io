About

Bridging human behavior and complex technical systems, turning experience into measurable signals and product decisions.

I'm a quantitative UX researcher with a mixed-methods toolkit. Most recently I was on Instagram's Reels Relevance team, translating user insights into priorities for the ranking system and into metrics the team could act on.

Before that I spent four years at Yale's Science of Well-Being, measuring how online well-being courses worked for more than 1.5 million learners. I hold a PhD in Psychology from Princeton, where I built computational models of how people predict each other's emotions.

My toolkit spans measurement design, A/B testing and causal inference, machine learning, SQL, Python, and R, alongside surveys and interviews. I also build AI-native workflows that cut repetitive analysis from hours to minutes.

Academics

In a previous life, I did experimental, computational, and neuroimaging research in academic psychology.

I did my PhD in the Princeton Social Neuroscience Lab, working mainly with Diana Tamir and Tom Griffiths on a Centennial Fellowship. My dissertation, Dyadic Emotion Prediction: Knowledge Structure, Accuracy, and Implications, asked whether people can refine general social knowledge to predict specific individuals, and what accuracy buys them. I paired probabilistic models with behavioral experiments, and used fMRI to study how thinking about other people reshapes our own self-knowledge.

Before that I earned a BA in Psychology (Honors, summa cum laude) at Vanderbilt, with minors in Quantitative Methods and Philosophy, doing clinical psychology research with Bunmi Olatunji and Sohee Park.

Collaborators: Sasha Brietzke, Lauren Do Feldman, Robert Hawkins, Elizabeth McDevitt, Meghan Meyer, Judith Mildner, Jordan Rubin-McGregor, Mark Thornton, Madalina Vlasceanu.
